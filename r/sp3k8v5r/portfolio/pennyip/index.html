Pennyip - Reclaiming Beauty, Restoring Craft, Redefining Value.

Penny Yip gave twenty years to the footwear industry: design and development, sourcing and production, factories and ateliers from Europe to Asia. Twenty years of knowing how a shoe is really made is what allows her to take one apart.

What she saw, she saw from inside. A shoe drawn for speed. Scaled for volume. Delivered without a single trace of the hand that made it. Somewhere in that acceleration, an object that had once been a storybook of its period — carrying its era's intention, emotion and cultural weight in a heel, a buckle, a line of gilding — became something with a season stamped on it.

Her answer is sculpture. Each Pennyip work is a single piece, and each is built the slow way, in the company of people who still know how: heels carved by hand, jewels set by master craftspeople, gilding applied by methods that predate the machine. She makes them with artisan houses including Massaro, alongside woodworkers, fine jewellers and 3D modellers — a circle assembled over two decades of knowing precisely who is left.

And the slowness is the argument. Put a hand-carved heel beside a moulded one and the case makes itself; no manifesto required. These works restore what the industry edited out, and in restoring it, they make its absence impossible to unsee.

The pieces are one-offs, made for collectors of art and of fashion, and shown through galleries, private sales and international art fairs. They sit exactly between haute artisanship and contemporary art, in a space nobody else has thought to claim — and each one keeps a vanishing technique alive a little longer, which may in the end be the most valuable thing about them.
