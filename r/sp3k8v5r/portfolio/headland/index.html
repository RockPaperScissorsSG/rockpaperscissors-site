Headland — the landing zone arrives with you.

A helicopter can reach almost anywhere. Landing is the harder problem. A pad has to be flat, load-bearing, clear of obstruction and — until now — built in place, which means concrete, permits and a permanent commitment to a spot that may only matter for a week.

Headland designs and manufactures retractable helipads for helicopters and UAVs, protected by an international patent. Closed, the unit takes the shape of a shipping container: it travels by road, rail or sea on standard handling equipment, and goes wherever a container can go. Open, it deploys into a certified landing surface on ground that had none.

That changes what counts as reachable. A ridge above a wildfire, a stadium car park on the night of an event, a stretch of coast that only needs air access for one season of the year — each becomes a landing zone for exactly as long as it needs to be, then returns to what it was. The range covers several landing surfaces, adapted to their setting and their intended use, and the units work across terrain rather than requiring it to be prepared first.

The operators are the ones for whom access is the mission. Fire services working ground no road reaches. Police and armed forces establishing a position and moving on. Event organisers and seasonal operations that need a pad for a fortnight and nothing at all for the rest of the year. In each case the same principle applies: the infrastructure travels to the situation.

Until now, a helicopter went where the ground allowed. Headland reverses the order: the ground follows the mission.
