LOVE - The World’s First Intelligent Turntable

You already know why you chose vinyl. The depth a groove holds, the ritual of the sleeve, the sound of a needle finding its place. LOVE keeps all three, and rebuilds everything around them.

It begins the moment the record goes down. A linear tracking sensor reads the disc — its diameter, its speed, how many tracks it holds — and connects to a database that names the album, the artist and every track on the side. The correct rpm is set without being asked. The sleeve appears on your screen. The record, for the first time, introduces itself.

From there the distance closes. Play, stop, skip a track, repeat a side, turn it up — all from your phone, from across the room, from the kitchen. Choose the third track by name, or tap the shell three times and let LOVE find its own way there. The music travels by Bluetooth or Wi-Fi to whatever is listening, Sonos included, or by cable to anything older. Leave the phone in another room and it plays just as well.

The object is the work of Yves Béhar: a pure lacquered line, light enough to carry from one room to the next, made to look no more dated in twenty years than it does now. Its design and mechanism are protected by patents granted in the United States and 150 countries — covering what LOVE does today, and what the platform is built to do next.

The market has been moving this way all along. Vinyl was worth $1.7 billion in 2022, and is forecast at $2.8 billion by 2028. The people behind that number want the record, the ritual and the sound — and a machine that meets them where they already live.
