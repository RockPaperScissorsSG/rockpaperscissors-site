Adding Years To Life, And Life To Years.

Indonesia is approaching an ageing population for the first time in its history. By 2030, an estimated 50 million Indonesians will be over sixty. Longer lives, better access to medical care and shifting family structures have created a demand the country has never had to meet at this scale.

Living Well Seniors Communities was built to meet it. The company opened Indonesia's first premium seniors day care, and has established itself as the leader of the country's seniors sector — a developer and operator working across specialist design and property development, hospitality, healthcare, leisure and property management, because a seniors community demands all of them at once.

Its clubhouses are the entry point. Designed around new skills, new friendships and better physical and mental health, they run days that move from fitness classes and social activities through educational workshops and wellness programmes, alongside all-day dining and chef-prepared meals. From there the portfolio extends into residences and a premium dementia village.

The work extends past the buildings. Living Well advises private and government institutions entering the silver economy, identifying land parcels for development, running market research and feasibility studies, and building partnerships with local providers including hospitals — the groundwork an industry needs before it can exist. Its president director co-founded the Asosiasi Seniors Living Indonesia, the sector's own association, making the company one of the builders of the industry it operates in.

That work has been covered by Bloomberg, Forbes, the Jakarta Post, Bisnis Indonesia, the Jakarta Globe, ABC Television, Business Week and Investor Daily, and the service has been recognised internationally.

What all of it amounts to is stated plainly by the company itself: we are changing the lives of seniors, delivering new hope, new friendships, new skills and new meaning.
