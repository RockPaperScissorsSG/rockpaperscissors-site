We make the films that carry it.

An executive production house strengthening East-West cultural ties through cinema.

Somewhere between Jakarta and the West, a conversation has been going on for decades — in music, in style, in the way a generation of Southeast Asians moves through the world. East West Synergy was founded on the belief that this exchange deserved to be filmed, at the scale and finish the subject warrants, and without softening a single frame for an audience abroad.

It began as a movement rather than a studio, which explains a great deal about the work. "Foxtrot Six" came first, an action feature built to the specifications of the genre and set entirely on Indonesian terms. Then followed "Susi Susanti: Love All," a portrait of the badminton champion, a figure every Indonesian knows and no film had yet honoured properly.

"Legacy of Java," directed by Budi Kurniawan, traces the relationship between people, land and Javanese coffee. It premiered in Jakarta before Indonesian ministries and the coffee industry, and has since screened in New York and Washington to introduce Indonesian coffee to the country that drinks the most of it.

Most recently, "ZsaZsa Zaturnnah," an animated feature that premiered at Annecy in 2026 — the most demanding room in the animation world.

EWS calls it hybrid cinema: developed and produced in the region, finished to travel, and made in the conviction that a film outlives almost every other instrument of influence a country possesses.
