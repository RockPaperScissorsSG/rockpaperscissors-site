Discover The Blue Mobility

There is a strange sliver of space, barely a few metres above the surface of the sea, where the laws of movement change. Air compresses between a wing and the water below it, forming a cushion that lifts and carries. Pilots have known about it for a century — most spend their careers trying to avoid it. Aqualines built a company on it.

A new generation of ultra-fast, low-carbon aquatic transportation.

The result is a class of craft the industry calls ground effect vehicles, and which Aqualines prefers to call naviplanes. It flies above the waters at speeds no boat can match, over routes no road will ever reach. The current generation reaches up to 320 km/h with a range of 500 km, in configurations from 2 seats to 300, driven by hybrid propulsion units designed for low carbon operation via electric, biofuel or hydrogen power.

Beyond limits.

What that unlocks is less a vehicle than a map. Every stretch of water becomes a corridor — no runway to build, no port to dredge, no bridge to finance. In an archipelago of more than 17,000 islands, or across the fractured coastlines of the Gulf and the Mediterranean, that is the promise of a revolution in mobility. It is a different geometry of access. And because the craft cruises above the surface rather than through it, it leaves the seabed, the biodiversity and the shoreline untouched — a sustainable connection through invisible roads, with no construction needed.

A new freedom of motion.

Embark anywhere. Every water surface becomes a high-speed way, open and unmetered, and the network extends as far as the water does. Ultra-fast aquatic mobility, running on routes that were always there, waiting to be used.
