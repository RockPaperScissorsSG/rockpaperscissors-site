Talent Connect - A Pathway Between Two Countries

Australia cannot staff its aged care sector. Indonesia graduates more nurses than its own system can absorb. Talent Connect closes the gap.

The programme takes qualified Indonesian nursing graduates and prepares them, over five months entirely in Jakarta, to work as licensed Personal Care Workers in Australian aged care. Candidates earn a Certificate III in Individual Support (Ageing) delivered through University of New England Partnerships, with educational resources and support provided by Binawan University. They complete their required aged care work experience in Living Well Seniors Communities' own facilities — which means the clinical placement happens in a working Australian-standard environment rather than a classroom. Alongside the qualification, workshops and seminars prepare them for something the certificate cannot cover: arriving in a new country and building a life there.

Selection matters as much as training. Candidates are screened for empathy and for a genuine commitment to caring for older people, on the reasoning that the technical skills can be taught and the disposition cannot.

The programme carries the backing of both governments. It operates under the Indonesia-Australia Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement, and in 2024 it received financial support from IA-CEPA ECP KATALIS, the bilateral economic cooperation programme, for the training of Personal Care Workers through the University of New England. The Australian Ambassador to Indonesia, Her Excellency Penny Williams PSM, marked the launch with a visit to the Living Well facility at Citra Garden City in West Jakarta.

Talent Connect grew out of Living Well Seniors Communities, incorporated in 2013 and the first licensed overseas provider of aged care in Indonesia. In 2023, responding to rising demand from overseas operators, the company began connecting Indonesian nurses to the Australian market, and the activity has since grown into a programme of its own.

What it builds is more durable than a placement. Each graduate is a healthcare pathway between two countries — a career for one person, a staffed shift for an Australian provider, and a set of skills that returns to Indonesia if and when they do.
