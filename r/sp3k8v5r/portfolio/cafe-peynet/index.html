Café Peynet - Les Amoureux Everywhere.

He drew the same two people for fifty years. A slight young man with a violin, a girl with a bouquet, sitting on a bench under a tree — Raymond Peynet's Les Amoureux became the way an entire country pictured being in love. They are in four museums, in Antibes and Brassac-les-Mines, in Karuizawa and Sakuto-cho in Japan. They stand in bronze in Hiroshima.

Café Peynet gives them a bench again.

The concept is a Parisian café-brasserie and a gallery at once, with permanent and temporary exhibitions of Peynet's work on the walls and his line — that instantly recognisable stroke — running through everything from the china to the awning. Parisian cooking, served through the day. A room designed for lingering rather than dining, and photographed accordingly.

What it sells is the 1950s as they were drawn rather than lived: sunlit, effervescent, tender, faintly absurd. A brasserie in that spirit is not a theme. It is a mood that has never really gone out of fashion, and a couple that has been sitting on that bench, waiting, since 1942.
