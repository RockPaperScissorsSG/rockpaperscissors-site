Neo Terra - Where Nature Sets the Rhythm.

In 1969, Edmond Lay built a hotel on the shore of Lake Payolle, at 1,100 metres. It was the only construction ever authorised on those banks, and it remains so. Lay, who would win the Grand Prix National d'Architecture in 1984, worked in the organic tradition: wood, concrete, slate, furniture and structure conceived as one composition — a building that reads as part of the landscape it sits in.

Payolle is known as the little Canada of the Pyrenees: one of the finest fir forests in the range, pastureland where cattle and wild horses graze, and an open view onto the Pic du Midi.

Neo Terra restores the building into a four-star hotel, restaurant and apartments, in a way the architecture can bear. Seven suites with private lake access, four apartment-chalets with terraces over the water.

The table celebrates the Pyrenean terroir — local, seasonal, refined — and it is deliberately more accessible than the rooms. Alongside it sit a café, a lounge, and food to take away for walkers passing on the trails. The intention is a house that works at two levels at once: exceptional for those who come to stay, open to those who live here.

There is a kind of luxury that cannot be built, only inherited: a lake with one house on it, and the silence of a fir forest in winter.
