The First Indonesian International Business Media & Market Watch.

Indonesia is the fourth most populous country on earth, the world's largest archipelago at more than 17,000 islands, and by 2045, on some projections, a candidate for the world's fifth-largest economy. Until Indonesia Business Post launched, none of that came with an English-language business press built for the executives, investors and entrepreneurs deciding whether to act on it.

That gap is the publication's premise. It positions itself as the country's first international business media and market watch — coverage aimed at a global audience as well as a domestic one, running alongside sections on markets and finance, corporate affairs, geopolitics, ASEAN and BRICS, defence and security, financial crime, and policy.

Indonesia's economy is already the seventh-largest in the world on a purchasing-power-parity basis, at $5.4 trillion on the IMF's projections for 2026, with growth that has held above 5% for years running. Add a population with a median age still in its twenties, and the case for coverage becomes less about potential and more about a market that has already arrived and is under-served by the press written for it.

The company frames the opportunity in the same terms: a market this size, growing this consistently, deserves coverage that matches its weight, and closing that gap doubles as a play for something less easily measured — the country's soft power, its standing with the global capital and diplomatic audiences the Post exists to reach.

Behind the newsroom sits a second business: due diligence and investigative research, business diplomacy advisory, media monitoring, and market-entry support.
