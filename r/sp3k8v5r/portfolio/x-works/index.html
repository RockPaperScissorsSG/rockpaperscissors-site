Disruptive Science: A Revolutionary Space Engine

Every spacecraft flying today moves by throwing something away. Fuel is burned and expelled, the vehicle is pushed the other way, and the mass that made the manoeuvre possible is gone for good. This is why satellites have finite lives, why every orbital change is budgeted like currency, and why a mission's ambition is fixed years in advance by how much propellant fits in the tank.

X-Works is patenting a way out of that constraint. Its infinite impulse space propulsion comes from a fine understanding of deep physics and a new interpretation of Newton's third law of motion, and it generates substantial thrust without rejecting any matter outside the spacecraft. There is no equivalent anywhere in the world, and the invention is the subject of international patents filed across fourteen jurisdictions — among them the United States, Europe, China, Japan, India, Russia and Canada.

Remove the fuel budget and the arithmetic of space changes completely. Orbit changes, including changes of orbital plane, would be free. A GTO to GEO transfer would take one week at zero mass cost. Constellation satellites would live fifteen years instead of five; Earth observation satellites, thirty instead of ten. Objects at the end of their service could be de-orbited and brought to a station for recycling, addressing the debris problem at its source rather than managing it. Launchers could impart a smaller initial impulse, cutting their own ecological footprint. And further out, the distances stop looking like distances: Mars and Jupiter within several weeks, the asteroid belt in about fifteen days.

The strategic approach to space would change radically. X-Works intends to be there when it does.
