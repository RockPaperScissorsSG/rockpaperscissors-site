I2M - Mushrooms Could Save The World.

Under every forest runs a network nobody sees. Fungi and trees have been trading water, nutrients and chemical signals through it for millions of years, and that exchange is much of what allows a forest to adapt and survive. I2M works on two consequences of that fact — one that goes back into the forest, one that comes out of the laboratory.

The Forest and the Table

The commercial mushroom industry rests on four sanitised strains whose yields fade after three years. I2M goes to the forest instead, cultivating wild strains never grown before, with terroir as the asset. Jacques Marcon, three Michelin stars, judges the results and will stamp the products. The same science replants forests with tree and fungus paired, so soil holds water through hotter years.

The Material

France bans polystyrene packaging in 2030, and I2M has spent five years growing a replacement from mycelium — light, moulded to any shape, tunable in texture and colour. SIPA, the country's largest polystyrene manufacturer, is running pre-industrialisation trials and will put it in front of its own customers.

Mycelium has not finished surprising anyone. Its acoustic, antibacterial and anti-combustible properties are still being explored, and industries whose markets are as large as packaging — construction, design — are already asking.

A forest's oldest technology, asked to do new work.
