Reinventing the art of giving, one experience at a time.

In a market saturated with gifts that get used once, forgotten in a drawer, or quietly re-gifted, KadoKita made a simple bet: what people remember isn't the object, but the moment.

Launched as Indonesia's first experience-gift platform, the company set out to replace the ritual of unwrapping with something harder to forget — a chocolate ritual at a Jakarta wellness retreat, a night of glamping on the cliffs of East Bali, a capoeira class that turns a Sunday into a story worth telling.

The idea is deceptively simple. Instead of choosing a product, the giver hands over a curated box of possibilities. The recipient picks the experience that speaks to them and books it themselves — turning every gift into an act of discovery rather than a transaction. It's a model built for the occasions that matter most: birthdays, weddings, anniversaries, farewells, the quiet milestones of corporate life that too often get marked with a card and little else.

That philosophy now spans two distinct audiences. On the corporate side, KadoKita has become a partner for companies rethinking talent loyalty — building customised recognition programmes that increasingly lean into social and sustainable impact, giving employee appreciation a purpose beyond the perk. On the consumer side, the platform has grown into a catalogue of more than 1,000 experiences across Indonesia, spanning fine dining, spa and wellbeing, staycations, and activities, refreshed seasonally for Valentine's Day, Christmas, and Eid al-Fitr.

Behind the offering sits a network of hundreds of hospitality and lifestyle partners — from boutique wellness retreats to hotel groups — each vetted to ensure the promise holds: choice, experience, and the kind of pleasure that lingers longer than any object could.
